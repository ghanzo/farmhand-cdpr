# Agronomic case

## Soil traffic and compaction

The strongest agronomic argument for Farmhand is reduced routine traffic over
cropped soil. Longepierre et al. reported that a compaction treatment increased
bulk density by 15%, reduced air permeability by 94%, reduced gas diffusion by
59%, and produced microbial-community changes that did not fully recover over
four growing seasons.

This does not mean the loosest possible soil is always best. Arvidsson's barley
study found reduced growth and nutrient uptake at both low and high bulk
densities relative to an intermediate condition. The design target is stable
aggregation and connected pore space with appropriate water, air, and root
access—not maximum looseness.

Farmhand should therefore measure:

- bulk density and penetration resistance;
- infiltration and gas transport;
- aggregate stability;
- root depth and architecture;
- microbial community composition and activity; and
- traffic intensity and spatial distribution.

## Diversified planting

Tamburini et al. synthesized 98 meta-analyses and reported that agricultural
diversification generally improved biodiversity, pollination, pest control,
nutrient cycling, soil fertility, and water regulation without reducing yield
overall. Outcomes and trade-offs remained context dependent.

Farmhand may expand the set of practical planting patterns because access no
longer depends on tractor clearance. That is an enabling mechanism, not proof
that any particular polyculture will outperform a local benchmark.

## Plant health and nutrition

The Advancing Eco Agriculture Plant Health Pyramid is a practitioner conceptual
framework covering photosynthesis, protein synthesis, lipid production, and
secondary metabolites. Farmhand can encode its claims as testable hypotheses,
but the framework should not be presented as scientific consensus.

Useful measurements include:

- sap and tissue mineral analysis;
- chlorophyll fluorescence and multispectral indices;
- Brix where appropriate;
- yield, marketable quality, and storage life;
- disease and insect incidence; and
- targeted secondary-metabolite assays.

## Trial design

The first agronomic trial should compare Farmhand-managed plots with a matched
management control. Record baseline soil variability, randomize treatments,
pre-register primary outcomes, retain raw sensor data, and report failures as
well as successes.
