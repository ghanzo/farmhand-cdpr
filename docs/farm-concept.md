# Farm concept

## Thesis

A cable-driven farm can decouple crop layout from the width, wheel tracks, and
turning radius of heavy machinery. That creates room for plant-level management
of diverse crops while sharply reducing routine axle traffic inside the growing
area.

The careful claim is not that rows disappear in every crop. Rows can still be
useful for light interception, irrigation, worker access, harvest logistics,
and competition management. Farmhand removes the requirement that crop layout
be designed around a tractor.

## Proposed system

The first Farmhand pilot covers a 10 × 10 metre plot with four towers. Eight
cables position a carrier above the canopy. The carrier supports:

- a rigid telescoping vertical stage;
- a short local robot arm or parallel wrist;
- perception and lighting;
- a tool changer;
- liquid and electrical connections; and
- a plant-level operations database.

Winches, high-mass power equipment, refill tanks, and the service dock remain
at the field edge whenever practical.

## Why it may be desirable

### Reduced field traffic

Conventional field traffic raises soil bulk density and can reduce connected
pore space, aeration, infiltration, and root access. Farmhand cannot eliminate
every soil disturbance, but it can keep routine machine weight on tower
foundations and service paths.

### Layout freedom

Plant-level access can support intercropping, relay cropping, living mulches,
flower strips, and spatial patterns that are awkward for tractor-mounted tools.
The benefit is management flexibility, not diversity by itself; crop selection
and spatial design still require agronomic testing.

### High capital utilization

One positioning infrastructure may carry sensing, spraying, sampling, pruning,
weeding, and harvesting tools. Electric ground-mounted drives may operate from
grid or on-farm renewable power and can work during long windows without moving
an engine across the plot.

### Continuous observation

Repeated plant-level imagery and samples can build a history of every plant,
operation, symptom, and response. This feedback loop may ultimately be more
important than autonomous motion alone.

## Central research question

Can a cable carrier provide enough safe workspace, stiffness, availability, and
throughput that a local manipulator can perform economically useful operations
without reintroducing heavy in-field traffic?

The simulator exists to turn that question into measurable constraints.
